## 8. References bibliografiche

Le reference principali sono VideoMAE v1 (NeurIPS 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12602), VideoMAE v2 (CVPR 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16727) e il repository upstream VideoMAE v2 (https://github.com/OpenGVLab/VideoMAEv2).

Comparing Vision Transformers and Convolutional Neural Networks for Image Classification: A Literature Review, J. Maurício, I. Domingues, J. Bernardino, https://www.mdpi.com/2225-1154/12/12/220.

A Comprehensive AI Approach for Monitoring and Forecasting Medicanes Development (2024), J. Martinez-Amaya, V. Nieves, J. Muñoz-Mari. Methods: Tracking: k-means clustering; Forecast: CNN + Random Forest. Dataset: 58 medicanes (1984–2023); Meteosat IR immagini (BT); reanalysis CERRA & ERA5 (MSLP, wind). First ML model dedicated to Medicanes. Detects cyclone location and structural features from IR images, with data-driven prediction of intensification. It can predict extreme medicanes events up to 2 days in advance with 65-80% accuracy, offering an innovative alternative to traditional models and adaptable to climate change. URL https://www.mdpi.com/2225-1154/12/12/220.

A Statistical Learning Approach to Mediterranean Cyclones (2025), L. Roveri, L. Fery, L. Cavicchia, F. Grotto. Methods: Unsupervised: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for dimensional reduction; Supervised: Statistical classifier on LDA feature. Dataset: ERA5 reanalysis (wind fields and pressure) + Mediterranean cyclone track archive. New Mediterranean cyclone classification workflow. LDA extracts cyclonic wind patterns by drastically reducing data size, then a classifier identifies cyclones and tracks. Result: ~90% accuracy in detecting Mediterranean cyclones using a few key features. The approach overcomes difficulties in defining shape/diameter of medicanes, implicitly identifying typical structures and paving the way for temporal precursors of extreme cyclones. URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15694v1.

Deepti: Deep-Learning-Based Tropical Cyclone Intensity Estimation System (2020), M. Maskey, R. Ramachandran, M. Ramasubramanian, I. Gurung, et al. Methods: CNN (custom model) for intensity regression (max wind). Dataset: IR GOES satellite images (15-min, multi-geostaz. 2000-2019); Best-track HURDAT2 (intensity). Automated intensity estimation from IR images. CNN learns the relationship between cloud patterns and maximum wind, emulating human (Dvorak) analysis. It obtains an RMSE error of 13.24 knots on wind speed, comparable to operational techniques. Provides objective estimates in near-real time and a visualization portal, representing one of the first fully data-driven systems for cyclone intensity assessment. URL https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9149719.

A Novel Deep Learning Based Model for Tropical Intensity Estimation and Post-Disaster Management of Hurricanes (2021), J. Devaraj, S. Ganesan, R. M. Elavarasan, U. Subramaniam. Methods: Enhanced CNN (batch normalization + dropout) for intensity; Transfer Learning: VGG-19 fine-tuning for damage and event classification. Dataset: IR GOES satellite images + HURDAT2 label (hurricanes 2000-2019); Post-event satellite images (e.g., Houston) for damage; Severe weather video for classification. Improves the state of the art in intensity and impact. Optimized CNN reduces intensity estimation error to 7.6 knots RMSE (about half that of previous models), accurately distinguishing the hurricane category. Moreover, by fine-tuning VGG-19 on impact data, it achieves 98% accuracy in predicting hurricane damage and 97% accuracy in classifying severe weather events. The integrated approach provides both more accurate forecasts and post-event information for disaster management. URL https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/9/4129.

Tropical and Extratropical Cyclone Detection Using Deep Learning (2020), C. Kumler-Bonfanti, J. Stewart, D. Hall, M. Govett. Methods: Segmentation (U-Net) - 4 U-Net variants to identify cyclone regions; label comparison from IBTrACS vs. heuristic algorithm. Dataset: Input: Global maps of total precipitable water (GFS 0.5°) and GOES satellite images (water vapor channel); Ground truth: IBTrACS (official TCs tracks) + heuristic detection of extratropical cyclones. Automated detection of cyclones on a global scale. U-Net models highlight cyclonic ROIs more quickly and accurately than traditional methods. They achieve 80-99% accuracy in marking cyclone regions (Dice coefficient 0.51-0.76), including many weak vortices ignored by manual approaches. The model for extratropical cyclones is 3 times faster than the operational reference algorithm. The use of multisource inputs (model + satellite) allows AI to discover “ambiguous” cyclones that escaped the strict criteria, improving coverage of alerted systems. URL https://colab.ws/articles/10.1175%2Fjamc-d-20-0117.1.

A Hybrid ML/Physics-Based Modeling Framework for 2-Week Extended Prediction of Tropical Cyclones (2024), X. Liu, et al. Methods: Hybrid model: coupling of a numerical model (WRF, ~2 km) with a global deep learning model (Pangu-Weather, ~25 km). Dataset: High-resolution WRF simulations + ML forecasts (Pangu); tests on real cases such as Cyclone Freddy (2023). Extends the forecast horizon for cyclones. By combining high-resolution physics with AI, the framework correctly anticipates cyclone trajectory and intensity up to ~2 weeks, compared to ~5 days for traditional models. In the case study on Freddy, the hybrid solution dramatically improves track and intensity over WRF or Pangu alone, extending accuracy from ~5 to 7 days and maintaining reliable forecasts over the entire 14-day period. This pioneering approach demonstrates that AI can enhance physics models, moving forward the limit of the state of the art in long-term forecasting. URL https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2024JH000207.

Tropical cyclone size estimation based on deep learning using infrared and microwave satellite data (2023), J. Xu, X. Wang, H. Wang, C. Zhao, H. Wang, J. Zhu. Methods: TC-ResNet: modified ResNet-50 (conv 5×5 on shortcut + dual attention canal/space) for wind radius regression. Dataset: Multi-satellite data: geostationary IR images and passive microwave maps (2003-2017) + global R34 wind ray dataset (best-track). Objective estimation of cyclone size (wind radius 34 kt). By integrating IR and microwave information, the model learns to filter out background noise and blurring in cyclonic clouds. Result: mean error 11.3 nm (≈21 km) on radius R34, with correlation coefficient 0.907, higher than traditional methods based on empirical parameters. The dual focus and multi-source data allow TC-ResNet to better capture the true wind extent, improving the risk assessment (wind, swell) related to cyclone size compared to the previous state of the art. URL https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.1077901/full.

Detecting Extratropical Cyclones of the Northern Hemisphere with Single Shot Detector (2022), M. Shi, P. He, Y. Shi, et al. Methods: Object detection (One-Stage): CNN Single Shot Detector (SSD) adapted to detect cyclone centers and phase. Dataset: Whole-disk (visible/IR) satellite images of the Northern Hemisphere; manually labeled dataset with cyclogenesis, maturity, and dissipation (criteria based on Bonfanti 2017). First application of SSD for extratropical cyclones. Innovative labeling and training pipeline enables the model to automatically detect low pressure centers in satellite images and classify their evolutionary stage. Achieves high performance: mAP 86.6% in recognizing mature cyclones and ~79.3% considering all three classes (developing, mature, fading). This demonstrates the ability of deep learning to reliably identify large-scale extratropical cyclones, which has been a complex challenge so far due to shape/size variability. The SSD model shows great potential for automating the monitoring of these storms in future weather operations. URL https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/2/254.
